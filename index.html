Why now

Winter is AGS's season. Here's the timing.

Life insurance isn't bought on a schedule — it's bought when something makes a family feel the gap. Three things do that between now and January.

Mortgage protection

A new house is the moment coverage stops matching the family

Every family that closed on a home this fall just took on the biggest debt of their life — and most left the bank with no idea whether their old policy covers a mortgage this size.

Opener: "You closed recently — quick question about how your family's protected now that the mortgage is real."
Year-end

December 31 is a real deadline for legacy planning

Year-end financial reviews, holiday family gatherings, and "get it done before the new year" thinking turn November and December into the strongest legacy-planning window of the calendar.

Opener: "Most families put this off until January 1st makes it urgent. Worth 15 minutes before the holidays do it for you?"
Young families

A child under 2 is the #1 life-event trigger

New parents buy life insurance more than any other group — but they're the least likely to have an agent they trust. They're searching, and whoever calls first wins the relationship.

Opener: "Congratulations — I work with young families on the piece most new parents miss."

Who I'd call

Same agency, three different conversations.

Your four services — mortgage protection, general life, wealth products, children's policies — map to three kinds of families. Each one gets a different opener, built around what they're actually feeling.

The new homeowner

Mortgage protection · your #1 service

Just closed, biggest debt of their life, group coverage through work that ends if the job ends. Feels relief about the house, anxiety about everything it now depends on.

First line they'd hear: "You just took on a mortgage your family depends on — most people's existing coverage doesn't come close to matching it. 15 minutes to check yours?"
Qualifying question: does their current coverage replace income through the mortgage term?

The young family

General life + children's policies

Kids under 5, one income matters more than it used to, no agent relationship. Searching online at 11pm — the warm lead AGS already buys.

First line they'd hear: "You asked about protecting your family — the part most young parents miss isn't the policy, it's how much. Here's the 2-minute version."
Qualifying question: who depends on the income, and for how many years?

The pre-retiree

Wealth products

Kids nearly gone, house nearly paid, thinking about what actually transfers to the next generation — and finding out taxes take the first cut.

First line they'd hear: "You've spent 30 years building this — the question most people your age haven't answered is what transfers, and what gets taxed on the way."
Qualifying question: is the goal income replacement, or legacy transfer?
